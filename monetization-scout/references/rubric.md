# Search patterns and ranking signals, per monetization type

Read the relevant section(s) before searching. Each gives: what query shapes
tend to surface real, current programs; what "leader in this niche" actually
looks like (so the ranking isn't just company size); and what payout signal
to look for.

## Affiliate programs

**Search patterns:** `"[niche/product category]" affiliate program`,
`site:[known player].com affiliate`, `best [niche] affiliate programs
[current year]`, `[niche] affiliate network` (to find aggregators like
ShareASale/Impact/PartnerStack/Rewardful hosting many relevant programs at
once — worth checking one of these networks directly for the niche).

**Leader signals:** category-defining product/service in searches and
review-site comparisons for the niche; frequently the first result when
someone searches "best [niche] tool/product"; visible in multiple
"best-of" roundups from independent sites, not just its own marketing.

**Payout signal:** commission % (recurring vs. one-time — recurring is
usually better even at a lower headline %), cookie/attribution window,
minimum payout threshold, and whether it's flat-CPA or revenue-share. A
program advertising "up to 50%" with no minimum-spend context is worth
noting as vague, not top-ranked on payout alone.

## UGC / creator deals

**Search patterns:** `[niche] UGC creator program`, `[brand] creator
partnership apply`, `[platform, e.g. TikTok/Instagram] creator fund
[niche]`, `[niche] brand ambassador program`.

**Leader signals:** an active, named creator program (not just "we love
working with creators!" copy with no application path); visible examples of
creators actually working with them (case studies, tagged content); a niche
where the brand is a top 3 name a creator in that space would recognize.

**Payout signal:** flat fee per piece of content vs. rev-share vs.
free-product-only — flag "free product only" clearly as the lowest tier, not
omit it, since it may still be worth listing for completeness.

## Sponsorships

**Search patterns:** `[niche] sponsor a [newsletter/podcast/video series]`,
`[niche] media kit sponsorship rates`, `[company] sponsorship inquiries`,
`[niche] industry event sponsorship`.

**Leader signals:** the sponsor (not the thing being sponsored) is a
recognized name buying attention in this niche repeatedly, not a one-off;
published media kits or rate cards are themselves a leader signal — a
company organized enough to have one is usually a serious, repeat sponsor.

**Payout signal:** this is usually inverted — the org is often the one
*seeking* a sponsor, not applying to be one. Note published rate cards from
comparable properties in the niche as a benchmark, and say plainly when no
rate is public (most aren't) rather than inventing a number.

## Partner / reseller programs

**Search patterns:** `[niche] partner program apply`, `[tool category]
reseller program`, `[niche] technology partner`, `become a [company]
partner`.

**Leader signals:** a formal tiered program (not just "reach out and we'll
see") with published requirements — tiering itself signals an established,
serious program.

**Payout signal:** revenue share tier, referral bonus structure, or
wholesale/reseller discount %. Note whether tiers require a minimum
commitment (deal volume, certification) — that's a real cost of entry, not
just a detail.

## Ebook / course marketplaces

**Search patterns:** `sell [niche] ebook/course platform`, `[niche] course
marketplace`, `best platforms to sell [niche] digital products`.

**Leader signals:** largest active buyer traffic/audience specifically
relevant to the niche (not overall platform size — a general platform with
poor niche-relevant traffic is not a leader here even if it's the biggest
platform overall).

**Payout signal:** platform fee/commission % taken from each sale, payout
schedule, and whether the platform brings its own buyer traffic
(marketplace-style, e.g. Gumroad's discover feed, Udemy) vs. is a pure
storefront tool with no built-in audience (e.g. a checkout widget you'd have
to drive your own traffic to) — this materially changes what "leader" means
for that platform.
